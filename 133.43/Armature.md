An **armature** is a structure of bonded [armana](./Armana.md). Armatures serve as a substitute material, as a program that automates thaumical acts, or as both. An armature complex enough to support a cerebrum is classed as autonomous.

## Bounce field and storage  
Every armature has been observed to produce a field with a radius of approximately 1.2 millimeters that bounces [raw mana](./Mana.md#Raw%20mana). A bounce changes only the direction of the raw mana and is believed to reset its lifetime (see [raw mana and armatures](./Mana.md#Raw%20mana%20and%20armatures)). The field persists for as long as the armature exists, including while the armature is decaying.  
  
Practitioners can store raw mana by building an armature. Raw mana under [manokinesis](./Manokinesis.md) is not bounced by the field, which allows it to be placed inside. Whether an armature draws upon its stored raw mana depends on the purpose for which it was programmed.  
  
## As a substitute material  
An armature may serve as a substitute for ordinary [matter](../120/Matter.md). It reproduces the material properties of the matter it replicates, with the exception of chemistry (see [material properties](./Armana.md#Material%20properties)). Conductivity is not regarded as chemical, so an armature replicating copper conducts current as intended, though it holds no energy of its own and must be supplied by a separate source.  
  
Because an armature is chemically inert, it does not corrode, rust, or react with its surroundings. This makes it well suited to marine infrastructure.  
  
The limits described in [Armana](./Armana.md#Stability) apply. A durable armature requires approximately 1 sextillion bonded particles in each piece, and an armature that splits may leave pieces that resume decaying. Powder and liquid armatures cannot be made without risking decay. Heating an armature to the melting point of the material it replicates turns it liquid, which is considered damage.  
  
Armatures can be joined to ordinary matter by gluing, slotting, nailing, or any other form of joinery.  
  
## As a program  
A programming language, such as Python, JavaScript, Java, C++, or C#, can be written on an armature through manokinesis to make it perform an automatic thaumical act. The language is written into the arrangement of the armature itself. A damaged program will still operate, but not as intended.  
  
An armature can reproduce how a [cogito](../120/Cogito.md) acts upon thaumics. It can convert [mana](./Mana.md) into [phana](./Phana.md), [manon](./Manon.md), armana, and matter. The armature is supplied according to its purpose: with manon to move objects, with light or phana to display, and with armana to build or program.  
  
A being may supply an armature with mana through manokinesis, either by the armature's program drawing the mana in or by the being projecting it toward the armature. In either case the armature, and not the being's cogito, performs the thaumical act.  
  
A staff is a practical example. It may consist of a wooden stick with a crystal at its top that is an armature replicating a crystal. When the wielder channels mana into it, the program produces the desired effect without the wielder performing the cogito's work.  
  
## Combined roles  
An armature can serve as both a substitute material and a program. The crystal of a staff replicates a crystal and carries a program. A shield may have an outer layer that is an armature, imitating a sturdy material while carrying a program that defends against attacks. Because the program is written into the same arrangement as the material, damage to the armature can degrade both.  
  
## Offensive and defensive programs  
Armature programs that use manokinesis follow the same rules as a practitioner (see [field interference](./Manon.md#Field%20interference)). A defensive armature may hold a manon field, supplied directly or converted from mana, and use it defensively.  
  
An offensive armature may project manon fields to interfere with the fields of others. A satellite, for example, may send multiple fields in stacked layers to a position on a surface. Because it destabilizes continuously, the time the fields take to travel is not a limitation. Such a program interferes with every manon field it contacts, including that of its own user, and leaves practitioners in the affected area unable to use manokinesis.  
  
## Pseudo-corpora  
An armature may run a continuous program of [corpus extension](../120/Corpus.md#Corpus%20extension), extending the boundary of a corpus around an ordinary object so that the object functions as the corpus of a being (see [pseudo-corpus](../120/Corpus.md#Pseudo-corpus)). The object is made of conventional materials, and the armature is the medium through which the being is placed in it. The assignment is an automatic program and does not require continuous effort from a practitioner.  
  
A being placed in an object that cannot support a complex cerebrum has a cogito of little consequence, and its anima gains little. Its anima may still supply mana.  
  
## Autonomous armatures  
An armature complex enough to support a cerebrum is classed as autonomous. The complexity may lie in the armature's code, its physical arrangement, or both (see [theory of complexity](../501/Theory%20of%20complexity.md)).  
  
The starting anima value of an autonomous armature depends on how long it takes to process or interpret the information it holds at emergence. The same applies to other beings with mechanical corpora.
