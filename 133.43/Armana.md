**Armana** (/ˈɑːr.mə.nə/) is a subatomic particle that can be bonded together to replicate matter. Structures formed by bonded armana are known as **[armatures](./Armature.md)**.

## Armagenesis  
The thaumical process for converting [mana](./Mana.md) into armana is known as **armagenesis**. Armana may also form through the spontaneous destabilization of raw mana. Raw mana does not otherwise convert into armana and will bounce off armatures instead of entering them.  
  
Armagenesis releases thermal energy as a byproduct. The amount released is fixed, and every armana created has been observed to release heat. Unlike the byproduct of [hylogenesis](./Hylogenesis.md), it cannot be adjusted. The heat is released into the surroundings, including the armature that is about to be built. The armana itself holds no energy, so the armature is warmed in the same way as any nearby object.  
  
## Lifespan and decay  
Released armana is metastable and decays within approximately 0.8 to 5 seconds, the same range as raw mana. [Manokinesis](./Manokinesis.md) acting upon an armana particle stops its decay for as long as control is maintained.  
  
Armana particles possess no intrinsic energy. When armana decays, it simply ceases to exist, without releasing a flash, heat, or force.  
  
## Bonding  
Bonding is the deliberate arrangement of armana particles into a structure. It is generally practiced through manokinesis, which stabilizes each particle before it is placed. Bonding is separate from armagenesis: armagenesis creates the particle, and bonding arranges it.    
  
Bonded armana still decays. Bonded particles behave like the atoms of a solid, and the loss of one particle affects only that particle. If the lost particle was the one holding the structure together, the armature will split, much as a metal rod is split by pliers. Damage and wear can also remove particles from an armature.  
  
There is no established limit on how many particles a practitioner can bond, or how quickly. This depends on the practitioner's skill and the technique used for bonding.  
  
## Stability  
The number of bonded particles in an armature corresponds to how long it can remain stable. An armature of approximately 1 sextillion (10<sup>21</sup>) particles is stable indefinitely. The threshold applies to each piece separately, so an armature that splits may leave pieces that resume decaying. Damage and wear can also strip an armature of particles.  
  
According to <img src="../Active%20armatures/Minormemorylossmandelbrot.jpg" width="12"> Ramanujan's information, an armature with one particle fewer than the threshold will decay in approximately 3.17 trillion years.  
  
Because of this, powder armatures and liquid armatures cannot be made without risking decay. Their grains or droplets each hold far fewer particles than the threshold.  
  
## Material properties  
Armana reproduces the material properties of the matter it replicates, such as structure, strength, and mass, with the exception of chemistry. Armana particles possess no intrinsic energy. A structure of armana replicating a radioactive element, for example, would not exhibit its radioactivity.  
  
Individual armana are invisible to the naked eye. An armature replicating glass appears as glass, including its transparency and reflection.  
  
## Armatures  
> Main article: [armatures](./Armature.md)  
  
An **armature** is a structure of bonded armana. Armatures can be joined to ordinary matter by gluing, slotting, nailing, or any other form of joinery. A staff, for example, may consist of a wooden stick with a crystal at its top that is an armature replicating a crystal.  
  
Armatures have been observed to bounce raw mana and to perform automatic thaumical acts when programmed. Their mechanics are described in the main article.
