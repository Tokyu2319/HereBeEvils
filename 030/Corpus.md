**Corpus** is the physical structure of a [being](./Being.md) and the means by which it interacts with, and [experiences](./Experience.md), the universe. It is the first constituent to emerge.  
  
## Classification  
Corpora are divided by origin. Natural corpora arise without deliberate construction and are grouped as faunal, floral, and mycelial. Artificial corpora depend on construction and are grouped as mechanical, synthetic, and armature*.  
  
## Corpus-corpus and corpus-cerebrum  
A corpus is generally described as having two parts. Its **corpus-corpus** is its physical body, including movement, sensory capacity, durability, tolerance of its environment, and compatibility with what it must interact with. Its **corpus-cerebrum** is the physical system on which [cogito](./Cogito.md) depends, such as neural tissue in biological corpora or processors in mechanical ones. The two are considered independent in sophistication but interdependent in function: the corpus protects the cerebrum, and the cerebrum directs the corpus.  
  
## The memories of a corpus  
The memories of a corpus refer to its stored information, which can be retrieved when needed. These memories may undergo alterations as the cerebrum is subjected to various psychological, cognitive, and environmental factors. The current memories of a corpus will replace any alterations made to the memories of a cogito without fail. If the corpus contains no relevant information, the memories of a cogito may supplement them if circumstances permit.  
  
## Corpus assimilation  
Corpus assimilation is when an object that suffices in [complexity](../501/Theory%20of%20complexity.md) naturally assimilates with a corpus. Observations suggest that this may occur either through the corpus actively incorporating the object or through a process requiring only sufficient compatibility between the object and the corpus. The medical procedure of transplantation is one example of corpus assimilation.  
## Corpus extension  
Corpus extension is the practice of expanding the defined boundary of a corpus through mana*, allowing external objects, another corpus, or hylogenized* matter to be temporarily treated as part of the user’s own corpus. The practice of corpus extension may assist in corpus assimilation. This principle is fundamental to many branches of thaumics:  
- Hylogenesis relies upon corpus extension to stabilize newly materialized solid matter.  
- External biogenesis* uses corpus extension to integrate damaged tissue into the practitioner’s corpus for reconstruction or restoration.  
- Advanced thaumaturgy may use corpus extension to temporarily govern external structures, tools, or environments as though they were extensions of the corpus itself.  
  
Without corpus extension, hylogenized matter often lacks sufficient structural continuity to remain stable, especially in the creation of rigid solids or complex biological structures.  
  
## Pseudo-corpus  
A pseudo-corpus may be assigned to an object that lacks sufficient complexity but is nevertheless forced to function as a being’s corpus, often through the use of armatures.
