**Experiences** are interpreted phenomena captured through the interaction between the [corpus](./Corpus.md) and the universe, processed by the [cogito](./Cogito.md), and resulting in the [anima](./Anima.md). The act of interpreting experiences refers to enrichment. Not all events become meaningful experiences. The value of an experience depends upon:  
- awareness,  
- interpretation,  
- novelty,  
- emotional significance,  
- and cognitive depth.  
  
An anima grows primarily through novel experiences. Repeated experiences provide diminishing contribution unless reinterpreted in a sufficiently different manner. Because interpretation itself may change over time, a previously understood phenomenon may generate additional value when perceived through a new perspective or level of understanding.  
  
## Experiential throughput  
The rate at which an anima accumulates its value is determined primarily by the complexity and capability of the [cerebrum](./Corpus.md#Corpus-corpus%20and%20corpus-cerebrum) supporting its cogito. More sophisticated cerebrums permit:  
- faster interpretation of phenomena,  
- deeper abstraction,  
- broader perception,  
- and greater reinterpretation of prior experiences.  
  
As a result, two beings exposed to the same event may derive vastly different experiential value from it.  
  
A being’s cerebrum possessing immense cognitive capability may rapidly accumulate value even within a limited environment, while a being with simpler cerebrum may require extensive time or exposure to achieve similar growth.  
  
However, cognitive capability alone does not guarantee unlimited growth. Novelty, interpretive variation, emotional significance, and environmental diversity remain necessary for meaningful experiential accumulation. Because of this, many highly intelligent beings eventually encounter a stopping point despite their advanced cognition. This particular phenomenon refers to experiential stagnation.  
  
## Comparative values  
These classifications are theoretical approximations used to describe the experiential maturity of animae. Though exact measurement remains impossible, thaumaturgists commonly categorize animae into broad ranges for comparative study. Anima value is denoted as ’AV’.  
  
- ~500 AV  
	- The emergence of sustained awareness and foundational cognition.  
	- Common among infants and simple-minded beings.  
  
- ~800 AV  
	- Capable of memory, social behavior, and adaptive interpretation.  
	- Common among primates and cognitively advanced animals.  
  
- ~2,500 AV  
	- Typical of educated adult humans living within stable modern environments.  
	- Growth often slows significantly due to experiential familiarity.  
  
- ~4,000 AV  
	- Associated with beings possessing broad intellectual exposure or prolonged cognitive activity.  
	- Includes multidisciplinary scholars, experienced thaumaturgists, and constrained artificial intelligences.  
  
- ~7,000 AV  
	- Represents centuries of accumulated reinterpretation and experiential continuity.  
	- Commonly associated with long-lived beings.  
  
- 50,000+ AV  
	- Animae possessing immense experiential density far beyond ordinary mortal development.  
	- Attributed to deities, cosmic intelligences, and certain transcendent entities.
