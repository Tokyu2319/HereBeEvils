A **hands-free program** is an [armature program](../133.43/Armature.md#As%20a%20program) applied to an object to maintain a [manon](../133.43/Manon.md) field on behalf of a practitioner. This allows the object to be held through [manokinesis](../133.43/Manokinesis.md) without occupying the practitioner's hands or requiring their focus to maintain the field. The program is written on an armature, which may be joined to the object by ordinary joinery (see [as a substitute material](../133.43/Armature.md#As%20a%20substitute%20material)) or may form part of the object itself.

## Operation  
The program maintains a manon field that holds the object on behalf of the practitioner. Unlike ordinary manokinesis, which requires the practitioner's focus to maintain a field, the program performs this act automatically. The object therefore remains suspended without requiring the practitioner to concentrate on holding it.  
  
The program does not necessarily manipulate the object beyond holding it. More complex actions require more complex code, and practitioners generally use their own manokinesis for such manipulation.  
  
Anyone capable of supplying mana can use the program's thaumical functionality. A person unable to supply mana can still hold and use the object conventionally.  
  
## Following  
The program may be configured with a range, allowing the object to follow a designated target when it moves beyond that range. The target need not be the practitioner and may be another person, an object, or anything else the program is configured to follow.  
  
## Applications  
A hands-free program may be applied to many kinds of equipment. The following are examples and do not constitute an exhaustive list of its applications.  
  
### Shield  
A shield held through a hands-free program leaves the practitioner's hands free to use other equipment, including two-handed weapons, heavy firearms, and staves. The program holds the shield and keeps it with the practitioner, but directing it against an attack remains a deliberate act of the practitioner's own manokinesis. Practitioners may also use multiple shields to intercept attacks from different directions (see [Shield (thaumaturgy)](../623/Shield%20(thaumaturgy).md)).  
  
### Weapons  
Weapons may be held without occupying the practitioner's hands, allowing them to carry additional weapons while using other equipment. This can make alternative weapons readily available without requiring the practitioner to physically carry or grip them.  
  
### Carrying equipment  
Backpacks, bags, and containers can follow their owners without being physically carried. This leaves the practitioner's hands free and reduces the physical burden of transporting equipment.  
  
### Surgical equipment  
Surgical instruments can be held and positioned without being touched by the practitioner. This helps preserve sanitation by reducing contact with equipment that must remain sterile. Instruments can also remain suspended within convenient reach, allowing the practitioner to access them without physically carrying or holding them.  
  
### Tools and instruments  
Tools can remain suspended near their users, allowing them to access multiple instruments without having to carry or put them down. This is useful in crafts, construction, maintenance, and other work involving several tools.  
  
### Transportation and cargo  
Packages, containers, and other cargo may be held and transported through manokinesis without requiring conventional carrying arrangements. The weight of the cargo affects how practical this is (see [limitations](Hands-free%20armature%20program.md#Limitations)).  
  
## Interference  
A hands-free program is subject to the same field interference as ordinary manokinesis (see [field interference](../133.43/Manon.md#Field%20interference)). Intruding manon fields destabilize the field holding the object, causing the manokinetic hold to fall.  
  
An object that falls in this way must be retrieved by its practitioner. An object held close to its practitioner drops nearby and is easy to recover. Increasing the distance between the object and its practitioner, such as by configuring it to follow from several metres away, risks interference.  
  
A program that interferes with every manon field it contacts, such as the offensive armature described in [offensive and defensive programs](../133.43/Armature.md#Offensive%20and%20defensive%20programs), causes every object held in this way within the affected area to fall at once.  
  
## Limitations  
The program requires mana to perform its thaumical act. An armature can store raw mana (see [bounce field and storage](../133.43/Armature.md#Bounce%20field%20and%20storage)), but whether its program draws upon that mana depends on the purpose for which it was programmed. Most hands-free programs are not built to do so, so the supply generally comes from the user.  
  
A hands-free program does not make an object independently capable of complex manipulation. Such functionality requires more complex programming.  
  
The number of manon particles required to hold an object affects the practicality of using the program. Holding a backpack, for example, requires fewer particles than holding a vehicle. Because interference may cause the object to fall and require retrieval, using the program on lighter objects can be more cost-efficient.  
  
A damaged program will still operate, but not as intended. The limits described in [armana stability](../133.43/Armana.md#Stability) also apply to any part of an object made of armature.
