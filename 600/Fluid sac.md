A **fluid sac** is a disposable, flexible container created through [hylogenesis](../133.43/Hylogenesis.md). It provides an enclosed environment in which a practitioner can perform [biogenesis](../133.43/Hylogenesis.md#Biogenesis) or create other forms of matter without having to maintain continuous corpus extension around the developing material.  
  
Fluid sacs are generally made from biodegradable polymers or natural films. The material is selected according to the intended application and the conditions in which the sac will be used. For example, a low-gravity environment places less mechanical stress on the container, allowing a cheaper and more fragile material to be used.  
  
## Creation and assimilation  
A practitioner creates a fluid sac directly upon the surface of their [corpus](../120/Corpus.md) using hylogenesis. It is not manufactured separately and subsequently attached. The sac’s size and shape are limited principally by the amount of [mana](../133.43/Mana.md) available to the practitioner.  
  
Upon creation, the sac is sterile, provided that its interior has not come into contact with the outside environment. The practitioner can first create and assimilate the sac, then introduce fluid into it through further hylogenesis. The fluid is created directly upon the sac’s interior surface; no opening or external filling process is required.  
  
Both the sac and its contents undergo [corpus assimilation](../120/Corpus.md#Corpus%20assimilation), becoming part of the practitioner’s corpus. [Corpus extension](../120/Corpus.md#Corpus%20extension) can accelerate the assimilation process. The fluid’s composition is determined by the intended application and the practitioner’s ability to formulate the appropriate substance through their biological discipline.  
  
## Applications  
The principal purpose of a fluid sac is to provide an environment for the creation of new matter without requiring the practitioner to devote continuous attention to extending their corpus around it.  
  
One important application is [lamination](../133.43/Hylogenesis.md#Techniques%20for%20solids): the creation of matter in successive layers within a fluid environment. The fluid provides a surface upon which hylogenesis can proceed. It may also provide structural support, although this is not always necessary when the developing matter is attached to existing tissue.  
  
For example, a practitioner who has lost an arm may create a fluid sac upon the stump. They can then use lamination to produce a replacement limb, progressively creating its constituent tissues from the point of attachment outward. The process may include the formation of bones, veins, nerve tissue and, finally, skin.  
  
Fluid sacs are not restricted to the creation of biological matter. They can also be used when creating nonliving substances that benefit from lamination within a fluid environment.  
  
## Thermal considerations  
When matter is created rapidly within a fluid sac, the surrounding fluid may become dangerously hot. Practitioners can deliberately reduce the [thermal byproduct](../133.43/Hylogenesis.md#Thermal%20byproduct), but doing so requires additional focus. Alternatively, they can control the rate of creation, allowing the process to proceed more slowly.  
  
Care must therefore be taken when performing hylogenesis within an enclosed volume of fluid. A slower, controlled process may be preferable to rapid creation.  
  
## Disposal  
Once the intended matter has been created, the practitioner tears apart the fluid sac and disposes of it. Its biodegradable construction and disposable nature make it unsuitable as a permanent addition to the corpus.
