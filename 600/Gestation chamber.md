A **gestation chamber** is an equipment used to house a [fluid sac](./Fluid%20sac.md) and imitate the environmental conditions required for extended gestation. Its primary purpose is to facilitate the creation and development of new [corpora](../120/Corpus.md), allowing a practitioner to determine the stage of life at which the corpus is produced, from infancy to adulthood.  
  
## Structure  
A gestation chamber resembles a pod, with an enclosed space that accommodates a fluid sac. It contains an outlet through which the practitioner creates the sac using hylogenesis. The outlet provides a connection between the practitioner’s corpus and the chamber, allowing corpus extension to establish the sac. The chamber also includes a separate outlet for extracting waste from the fluid sac.  
  
Library Alhazen‘s gestation chambers are additionally equipped with instruments alongside the outlet. These instruments facilitate somatic cell nuclear transfer and memory uploading. Their positions can be adjusted using the chamber’s computer.  
  
## Nutrient supply  
The chamber provides a means of supplying nutrients to the fluid sac. Nutrients may be supplied from a reservoir or delivered from an external source such as nutrient bags or [biogenesis](../133.43/Hylogenesis.md#Biogenesis).  
  
The nutrient outlet may also connect to an umbilical cord attached to the developing corpus. The umbilical cord is created as part of the initial biogenesis. As the corpus develops, the nutrient outlet must accommodate its growth. It may begin at a smaller size and require expansion as the corpus reaches later stages of development, such as pubescence.  
  
## Environmental support and monitoring  
The chamber imitates the environmental conditions required for extended gestation. It can provide heat and regulate other conditions within the fluid sac.  
  
An ordinary computer assists with monitoring and regulating the process. It can monitor:  
- The developing corpus, including its heart rate, growth, movement, anatomical development, blood circulation, and developmental stage.  
- The gestational environment, including temperature, fluid composition, oxygen availability, pH, nutrient concentration, waste accumulation, fluid volume, pressure, and contamination.  
- The chamber’s own systems, including nutrient delivery, waste extraction, the condition of the fluid sac, the umbilical cord, and the operation of its equipment.  
  
The computer can also make adjustments as required, including regulating temperature and nutrient delivery, controlling waste extraction, and adjusting the dimensions of the nutrient outlet to accommodate the developing corpus.  
  
## Operation  
The practitioner establishes contact with the chamber through an input such as a handle. Corpus extension is used to create the fluid sac through the chamber’s designated outlet. Once the sac has been created, the practitioner may disconnect; continuous corpus extension is not required throughout gestation.  
  
The fluid sac’s innate sterility upon creation makes it suitable for biogenesis. The chamber therefore does not need to create the sac through any other means. Biogenesis may also be used to provide nutrients, although practitioners generally prefer to use available nutrient supplies rather than spend their own mana or draw from a mana store for this purpose.  
  
Once the sac has been established and the developing corpus is supplied with nutrients, the chamber maintains the conditions required for extended gestation. Its computer monitors the process and adjusts the equipment as the corpus develops.
