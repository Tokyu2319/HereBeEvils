A **hydrostatic hylogenesis chamber** (**HHC**) is an industrial equipment used to create large solids through [hylogenesis](../133.43/Hylogenesis.md). It consists of a chamber filled with a fluid medium, in which solids are created by [lamination](../133.43/Hylogenesis.md#Techniques%20for%20solids), with the medium acting as a fluid [corpus](../120/Corpus.md#Corpus%20extension).

## Structure  
An HHC is commonly a box-shaped chamber, whose body is made entirely of conventional material such as steel. Part of the chamber is kept as a vacuum to accommodate new material, so that the pressure of the medium does not build up as a solid forms. The chamber has an opening through which finished solids are removed.  
  
The thaumical functions of an HHC are provided by one or more [armature programs](../133.43/Armature.md#As%20a%20program) [joined to the chamber](../133.43/Armature.md#As%20a%20substitute%20material). Beyond the chamber itself, an HHC includes a contact point for the practitioner and a mana store.  
  
## Medium  
The chamber is filled with a fluid medium in which lamination takes place. Pure water may be used, but the best results are obtained with perfluoropolyether fluids, which are thermal and fire resistant, chemically inert, and resistant to radiation.  
  
An HHC cannot be used for [biogenesis](../133.43/Hylogenesis.md#Biogenesis), which requires a more sterile medium.  
  
The [thermal byproduct](../133.43/Hylogenesis.md#Thermal%20byproduct) of hylogenesis is borne by the chamber and its medium. The practitioner's own corpus is not at risk of harm.  
  
## Corpus extension and the contact point  
One armature program maintains corpus extension continuously between the practitioner, the chamber, and the medium inside it, so that the practitioner's corpus becomes one with the chamber. Because corpus extension is maintained for them, the practitioner can devote all of their focus to hylogenesis.  
  
The practitioner must be connected to or in contact with the chamber for corpus extension to work. The contact point may take the form of a handle, a handprint plate, a glove or gauntlet, a grip rail, a footplate, a seat, or any other form of contact that allows corpus extension and hylogenesis.  
  
## Assisting programs  
An HHC may carry an additional armature program to assist with hylogenesis. Assisting programs vary between equipments. They may:  
- automate hylogenesis when given an instruction to construct a desired solid;  
- reduce or introduce heat through [manokinesis](../133.43/Manokinesis.md);  
- shape the solid during hylogenesis;  
- perform other functions.  
  
Armature computers may also be used to construct a desired solid, which allows the practitioner to work with little focus.  
  
## Production cycle  
A typical cycle proceeds as follows:  
1. The practitioner establishes contact with the chamber, and corpus extension is maintained.  
2. Hylogenesis takes place within the medium, with the vacuum accommodating the new material.  
3. When the solid is finished, the vacuum is released and the medium is drained or siphoned.  
4. The opening of the chamber is opened, and a vehicle or an instrument is used to remove the solid.  
5. The chamber is closed and returned to a vacuum, and the medium is put back for later use.  
  
## Mana supply  
The chamber can be built to large dimensions, but the size of the solid it can produce is limited by the practitioner's mana. Most HHCs therefore include a mana store, a separate device that is part of the HHC and supplies mana to the equipment. Its form varies, and may resemble a tower, a silo, or something smaller such as a industrial batteries.  
  
## Limitations  
The size of a solid is limited by available mana and not by the dimensions of the chamber.  
  
An HHC cannot perform biogenesis.  
  
The practitioner must remain connected to the chamber for corpus extension, even when armature computers reduce the focus required.  
  
A damaged program will still operate, but not as intended. The limits described in [armana stability](../133.43/Armana.md#Stability) also apply to any part of an HHC made of armature.
