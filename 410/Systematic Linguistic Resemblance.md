**Systematic linguistic resemblance** is a phenomenon in which languages exhibit similarities across various linguistic aspects despite not necessarily having any historical or geographical relationship. The extent of these similarities varies considerably, with some languages being almost identical while others share only a limited number of characteristics. The phenomenon has been observed between languages originating from different worlds, although its underlying cause remains unexplained.  
  
## Linguistic resemblance  
Resemblance between languages may occur across several linguistic aspects, including phonetics, phonology, grammar, and vocabulary. These similarities do not necessarily occur together or to the same extent. Two languages may exhibit considerable similarities in their phonetics while differing substantially in their grammar and vocabulary.  
  
Some languages are almost identical in several linguistic aspects, whereas others exhibit only limited similarities. Resemblance in vocabulary is less common than that in other linguistic aspects.  
  
Similarities may also occur between writing systems, although their extent may depend on the medium used for writing.  
  
Despite these similarities, speakers of different languages are not necessarily able to understand one another without learning the respective languages. Nevertheless, the resemblance can make language acquisition and translation considerably easier.  
  
## Observations  
Library Alhazen has observed systematic linguistic resemblance between languages originating from different worlds, including those without any known historical or geographical contact. No apparent pattern governing which languages exhibit similarities has been identified.  
  
The Library’s observations of languages across multiple worlds are ongoing. Consequently, its current documentation does not establish the full extent or distribution of the phenomenon.  
  
## Hypotheses  
Library Alhazen theorizes that humanoid species independently produce many of the same sounds. This may account for some of the phonetic similarities observed between their languages.  
  
However, this hypothesis does not necessarily account for similarities in other linguistic aspects, such as grammar and vocabulary. No explanation encompassing the entire phenomenon has been established.  
  
## Applications  
Systematic linguistic resemblance provides practical advantages for communication between inhabitants of different worlds. Travelers who speak one language may find it easier to learn another when the two exhibit considerable similarities.  
  
The phenomenon is also beneficial to Library Alhazen’s archival activities. Similarities between languages facilitate the translation of its collection into different languages, reducing the difficulty associated with making its contents accessible to speakers of those languages.
