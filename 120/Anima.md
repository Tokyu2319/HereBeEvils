**Anima** is understood to be the result of [enrichment](./Experience.md) and the source of [mana](../133.43/Mana.md). It is the last constituent to emerge, forming once the [cogito](./Cogito.md) is able to interpret experiences. Animae do not obey the laws governing matter and energy, only interacting with the universe through thaumaturgy.  
  
## Mana exhaustion  
Mana exhaustion occurs when a being depletes its internal mana reserves beyond sustainable limits. Because mana contributes to the cohesion between the cogito, corpus, and anima, severe depletion may destabilize the integrity of the three constituents themselves. Early symptoms commonly include:  
- Physical weakness  
- Cognitive impairment  
- Emotional destabilization  
- Sensory distortion  
- Diminished awareness of self  
  
Extreme exhaustion will result in decoherence of the three constituents without necessary harm to neither the corpus or the cogito. Decoherence will generally result in death.  
  
### Mana exhaustion blindness  
Paradoxically, beings whose anima are of higher value, are often more vulnerable to catastrophic exhaustion despite their immense mana reserves. The vast scale of their reserves may obscure awareness of depletion until decoherence has already begun.  
  
This blindness cannot be felt by higher beings; it can only be known indirectly, by those who have studied how their own depletion behaves. Those who possess this understanding tend to release their mana deliberately, stopping short of the threshold they cannot sense. Those who do not remain wholly unaware of how close they stand to it—and may discover the limit only by crossing it, pouring their entire reserve into a single act and collapsing dead the instant it is spent, with no warning ever having reached them.  
  
## The memories of an anima  
The memories of an anima refer to the information it holds of the cogito it was built through. In transience, an anima retains these memories, and they are the source of the memories restored by [cogito resurrection](Anima.md#Cogito%20resurrection).  
  
A transient anima is unable to gain experiences, so its memories do not change while it remains in transience.  
  
## Transience  
Transience is a state where an anima is without its cogito and [corpus](./Corpus.md). Transience is a natural state associated with death, considered a form of afterlife. Animae that exist in this state are referred to as transient anima. In this state, transient animae are unable to gain experiences and, because animae do not obey the laws governing matter and energy, their position will remain fixed in space until a thaumical disturbance alters it.  
  
## Cogito resurrection  
**Cogito resurrection** is the thaumical restoration of a cogito, together with its memories and continuity, from a transient anima. The restored cogito is the same individual who previously existed, and not a successor.  
  
### Requirements  
Cogito resurrection requires an anima in transience, an available corpus, and one or more donor animae of equal total value to the anima being restored. The memories of the restored cogito are held to originate from the transient anima.  
  
### Donors  
Donor animae are transient animae. They are consumed and destroyed in the procedure. Cogito resurrection is the only known method of destroying an anima.  
  
### Distinction from replacement  
When a cogito is lost while its corpus remains intact, the corpus may give rise to a new cogito, which is a separate individual (see [loss and replacement](./Cogito.md##Loss%2520and%2520replacement)). Cogito resurrection differs in that it restores the original cogito.
