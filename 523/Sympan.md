> [!note] *The information presented in this article is information provided from █████-████.*  
  
A **sympan** is a domain of reality distinguished from neighboring domains by differences in their histories and macroscopic states. The universe is understood to comprise an immense number of such sympanta, each encompassing a range of minor variations that collectively correspond to a dominant macroscopic state. Sympanta are not described as separate universes governed by different physical laws, but as distinct domains within a single universe. Observations made during intersympantic travel are consistent with this description.  
  
Sympantic boundaries are associated with substantial divergences in the histories or macroscopic states of reality. The degree of similarity between neighboring sympanta progressively decreases with increasing sympantic distance, reflecting the accumulation of historical and macroscopic divergences. Minor variations do not distinguish separate sympanta, whereas more substantial differences, such as alternate historical developments or the existence of counterparts to familiar individuals, indicate that two observations originate from different sympanta. The precise criteria by which these divergences are classified have not been established.  
  
Sympanta are accessible through intersympantic travel. A distance of approximately 10<sup>10<sup>72</sup></sup> meters is given as the scale at which a neighboring sympan is reached, though the relationship between this distance and the geometry of sympantic boundaries remains unexplained. Travelers entering neighboring sympanta have encountered versions of familiar individuals and worlds whose histories differ from those of their sympan of origin. The extent of these differences is observed to increase with sympantic distance.  
  
## Counterparts  
A **counterpart** is an individual encountered in another sympan who bears sufficient similarities to an individual from the observer’s sympan of origin to be considered a corresponding version of them. Such similarities may include names, personal characteristics, and historical circumstances, although no definitive criteria for establishing counterpart identity have been established. Classification is therefore dependent on the observations and assessments of the individual making the identification.  
  
Neighboring sympanta contain counterparts of individuals from their adjacent domains, owing to the relative proximity of their respective histories and macroscopic states. However, the degree of similarity between corresponding individuals progressively decreases with sympantic distance, reflecting the increasing divergence between their respective histories and states of reality. Beyond a certain degree of divergence, individuals may bear insufficient resemblance to those of the observer’s sympan of origin to warrant counterpart classification. Such individuals are regarded as individuals originating from substantially divergent sympanta rather than counterparts.  
  
Library Alhazen observers have recorded encounters with 14 counterparts of individuals from their sympan of origin. This figure represents the number of counterparts encountered during documented intersympantic observations and should not be interpreted as a universal limit or an exhaustive account of their occurrence throughout the universe. The total number of counterparts belonging to any individual remains undetermined.
