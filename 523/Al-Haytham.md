**Al-Haytham** is a K-type main-sequence star located at the centre of the Al-Haytham planetary system. It has a stellar classification of K2V and hosts three confirmed planets, designated Al-Haytham b, Al-Haytham c, and Al-Haytham d, as well as an artificial megastructure known as the Library Alhazen. The star has a mass of 0.82 solar masses, a radius of 0.79 solar radii, and an effective temperature of 5,100 K. Its luminosity is 0.38 solar luminosities, and its estimated age is 6.8 billion years.  
  
Al-Haytham is an orange dwarf star with a relatively low stellar mass and luminosity. It belongs to the main sequence, where it generates energy through the nuclear fusion of hydrogen into helium in its core. Its planetary system consists of three planets with distinct orbital characteristics, ranging from a relatively close-in terrestrial planet to a distant gas giant. The system also contains an artificial megastructure orbiting within the circumstellar habitable zone.

## Stellar characteristics  
Al-Haytham is classified as a K2V star, placing it within the K-type main sequence. Its orange coloration results from its relatively low photospheric temperature compared with hotter main-sequence stellar classes. The star has an effective temperature of approximately 5,100 K and a photospheric radius of 550,000 km.  
  
The star has a mass of 0.82 M☉ and a surface gravity of approximately 4.56 (log g, cgs). Its luminosity is 0.38 L☉, corresponding to a bolometric magnitude of 5.79. The star’s photosphere exhibits a predominantly orange spectrum, with strong absorption features characteristic of late-G and early-K stellar atmospheres.  
  
Al-Haytham has a metallicity of [Fe/H] = +0.08, indicating an iron abundance approximately 20 percent greater than the adopted reference abundance. Its chemical composition is dominated by hydrogen and helium, with heavier elements accounting for approximately 2 percent of its total mass.  
  
The star rotates with a period of 31.6 days and has a projected rotational velocity of 1.3 km/s. Its chromospheric activity is relatively low, consistent with its age and rotational characteristics. The star’s magnetic field produces periodic variations in surface activity, including localized starspots and occasional stellar flares.  
  
Al-Haytham is estimated to be 6.8 billion years old. Its relatively low mass contributes to a prolonged main-sequence lifetime, allowing it to sustain hydrogen fusion over several tens of billions of years.  
  
## Planetary system  
The Al-Haytham planetary system contains three confirmed planets and an artificial megastructure orbiting the central star. Its planetary arrangement includes an inner terrestrial planet, a temperate terrestrial planet, and an outer gas giant. The system's artificial megastructure, Library Alhazen, occupies an independent orbit within the habitable zone.  
  
The planets are designated **Al-Haytham b**, **Al-Haytham c**, and **Al-Haytham d**, following the conventional astronomical designation system in which the lowercase letter b is assigned to the first discovered planet.  
### Al-Haytham b  
Al-Haytham b is the innermost planet in the system. It is a terrestrial planet with a mass of 0.84 Earth masses and a radius of 0.94 Earth radii. It orbits Al-Haytham at a mean distance of 0.19 AU, completing one revolution every 33.4 days.  
  
The planet has an orbital eccentricity of 0.04 and an orbital inclination of 1.8° relative to the system’s reference plane. Its equilibrium temperature is approximately 490 K, resulting from its proximity to the host star.  
  
Al-Haytham b has a predominantly silicate composition with a metallic iron core. Its surface is characterized by extensive volcanic plains, impact craters, and large basaltic formations. The planet possesses a thin atmosphere dominated by carbon dioxide and nitrogen, with traces of sulfur dioxide.  
  
### Al-Haytham c  
Al-Haytham c is the second planet in the Al-Haytham system and the largest of its two terrestrial planets. It has a mass of 1.16 Earth masses and a radius of 1.07 Earth radii. Its semimajor axis is 0.61 AU, corresponding to an orbital period of 192.7 days.  
  
Al-Haytham c possesses a substantial atmosphere composed primarily of nitrogen, with argon, carbon dioxide, and water vapor as secondary constituents and only trace free oxygen. Its atmospheric pressure averages 1.08 bar at the reference surface level. The planet has extensive liquid-water reservoirs, including oceans and inland seas, as well as continental landmasses. Its equilibrium temperature is approximately 257 K, with a mean surface temperature near 288 K owing to the atmospheric greenhouse effect.  
  
Despite its stable climate, Al-Haytham c has no native biosphere. Its continents are barren, with no soils, and its oceans are biologically sterile. It is the principal habitable world of the system.  
  
### Al-Haytham d  
Al-Haytham d is the outermost confirmed planet in the system. It is a gas giant with a mass of 0.71 Jupiter masses and a mean radius of 0.96 Jupiter radii. It orbits the host star at a semimajor axis of 2.84 AU, completing one revolution every 1,928 days.  
  
The planet has an orbital eccentricity of 0.07 and an orbital inclination of 1.4°. Its atmosphere consists primarily of molecular hydrogen and helium, with methane, ammonia, and water vapor contributing to its atmospheric chemistry.  
  
Al-Haytham d has an extensive system of natural satellites and a faint ring system composed of dust and icy particles. Its atmosphere exhibits prominent cloud bands and large-scale atmospheric circulation, producing alternating regions of high- and low-pressure weather systems.  
  
### Library Alhazen  
> Main article: Library Alhazen  
  
Library Alhazen is an artificial megastructure located within the habitable zone of the Al-Haytham system. It occupies an independent orbit around the host star, situated within the same general orbital region as Al-Haytham c.  
  
The structure serves as a major research and archival institution, housing extensive collections of scientific records, historical documents, astronomical observations, and other forms of accumulated knowledge. It is the originating institution of the information documented in this article.  
  
## Habitable zone  
The circumstellar habitable zone of Al-Haytham extends approximately from 0.58 to 0.85 AU, based on the star’s luminosity and adopted conservative habitable-zone boundaries.  
  
Al-Haytham c orbits within this region at a mean distance of 0.61 AU. Its atmospheric properties and surface conditions support the presence of stable liquid water, making it the principal habitable planet in the system.  
  
The relatively low luminosity of Al-Haytham places its habitable zone closer to the star than the corresponding region around higher-mass main-sequence stars. Its extended stellar lifetime provides a prolonged period of relatively stable energy output for the planetary system.  
  
## Formation and evolution  
Al-Haytham formed approximately 6.8 billion years ago from the gravitational collapse of a molecular cloud enriched with heavier elements from previous generations of stars. The surrounding protoplanetary disk provided the material from which its planetary system developed.  
  
The three planets formed through the accumulation of solid material and subsequent gravitational accretion. The terrestrial planets developed within the inner regions of the protoplanetary disk, while Al-Haytham d accumulated a substantial gaseous envelope beyond the system’s inner planetary region.  
  
Following the dissipation of the protoplanetary disk, the planetary system underwent orbital evolution and long-term dynamical stabilization. Its present configuration consists of three planets with relatively low orbital eccentricities.  
  
Al-Haytham continues to generate energy through core hydrogen fusion and remains in the main-sequence phase of stellar evolution.  
  
## Future evolution  
Al-Haytham will continue its main-sequence evolution as hydrogen is converted into helium within its core. As its core hydrogen abundance decreases, its luminosity and radius will gradually increase.  
  
Following the exhaustion of core hydrogen, the star will leave the main sequence and expand into a red giant. Subsequent evolution will involve helium fusion, followed by the ejection of its outer atmospheric layers and the formation of a planetary nebula.  
  
Its remaining stellar core will eventually become a white dwarf composed primarily of carbon and oxygen. The planetary system will undergo substantial changes during these evolutionary stages, particularly in response to the star’s increasing luminosity and subsequent mass loss.
