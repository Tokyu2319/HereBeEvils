**Alima** (/ʕaˈliː.ma/) is the calendar system used by Library Alhazen for the measurement and organization of time. Its epoch begins with the founding of Library Alhazen, with the year of its establishment designated as Alima 1.  
  
The calendar consists of 360 days divided into twelve months, each containing 30 days. A standard day consists of 24 hours, while each week consists of six days. The institutional work schedule follows a five-day workweek followed by one designated rest day.  
  
The Alima calendar recognizes four seasons, each corresponding to a period of three months. Its standardized structure is maintained through the Library’s ability to regulate its orbital and rotational characteristics.  
  
Alima serves as the principal system of chronological reference within Library Alhazen and is used in institutional records, administrative activities, documentation, and other operations.  
  
## Calendar structure  
The Alima calendar consists of 360 days distributed evenly across twelve months. Each month contains 30 days, resulting in a uniform calendar structure without variations in monthly length.  
  
A year is divided into four seasons, each comprising three consecutive months. The calendar does not require additional days or intercalary periods to maintain its standard annual duration.

## Seasons  
The year is divided into four seasons, each containing three months and lasting 90 days.  
Each season corresponds to one quarter of the calendar year.  
  
## Weeks and working days  
A standard Alima week consists of six consecutive days. The institutional work schedule designates five days for regular work activities, followed by one rest day.  
  
This arrangement produces 60 complete weeks within a standard calendar year, comprising 300 scheduled working days and 60 designated rest days.  
  
## Timekeeping  
A standard day in the Alima calendar consists of 24 hours. The duration of a standard hour follows the conventional division of an Earth-standard day into 24 equal periods.  
  
The Library’s ability to regulate its orbital and rotational characteristics allows it to maintain the conditions necessary for standardized daily timekeeping.  
  
## Calendar epoch and dating  
The Alima calendar begins with the founding of Library Alhazen. Its first year is designated Alima 1, with subsequent years numbered consecutively.  
  
The calendar is used to establish chronological references for institutional events, records, administrative decisions, and other documented occurrences.  
  
Dates may be expressed using the year, month, and day of the Alima calendar. The year designation is commonly used independently when a more specific date is unnecessary.  
  
## Institutional use  
Alima is the principal calendar used throughout Library Alhazen. Its standardized structure provides a common chronological reference for the institution’s personnel, departments, activities, and archival records.  
  
The calendar’s regular monthly, seasonal, and weekly divisions facilitate the organization of institutional schedules and the preservation of chronological consistency across the Library.
